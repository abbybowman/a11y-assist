# 3.A Flashing: research before a spec (2026-11-03)

Research for Abby, requested after the 2.B color-change decision.
Flashing is the fast end of the same problem 2.B handles (Understanding
2.2.2: blinking faster than 3 per second "would also be considered a
flash"), and it needs the same tools as the 2.x batch. This note is
research, not an authority. The TT PDF is. Experiments are in
[2026-11-03-2x-experiments/](2026-11-03-2x-experiments/README.md) (the
`flash_*` scripts).

**Bottom line.**

- **The TT does not test flashing.** It records NOT TESTED whenever
  flashing is found, "until … a testing tool is identified". It says a
  tool "would be very helpful but is not available at this time".
- **No scanner, ACT rule, Baseline Alignment case or DHS verdict covers
  2.3.1.** The tools that do (PEAT, Harding FPA, EA's IRIS) analyze
  recorded video, not web pages.
- **We can measure web-page flashing exactly**, by stepping the page's
  clock and animations frame by frame, and by decoding images and video
  frame by frame. Real-time screenshots are not safe: a strobe was read
  at 1–2.5 flashes per second instead of about 30, which would look like
  a PASS.
- **Building it is mostly pass T plus a frame-stepping capture.** The
  decision Abby needs to make is what outcome to give, since the TT text
  names NOT TESTED (O1 below).

## 1. What the TT says (pp. 18–19)

> Visually identify any content that flashes. Flashing is content that rapidly alternates between two or more states that vary significantly in contrast.
> When flashing content IS found, the result for Test ID 3.A, 2.1.3-flashing is NOT TESTED.

The test condition reads: "If NO flashing content is found, then this
Test Condition DOES NOT APPLY (DNA). If flashing content IS found, then
this test should be recorded as NOT TESTED."

The note reads: "Multiple requirements are specified for conforming
flashing content. To determine if requirements are met, a testing tool
would be very helpful but is not available at this time. The test process
will be updated when a testing tool is identified. Until then, the result
'Not Tested' will indicate that flashing content was found."

Applicable Standards: "Until such time that this test process includes a
test for flashing content, no definitive statement can be made regarding
Conformance Requirement 5 if any flashing content is present."

So 3.A is the one test whose TT text names NOT TESTED, and CLAUDE.md
principle 2 allows it only there. The TT never gives 3.A a PASS or a
FAIL.

## 2. WCAG 2.3.1 (Section 508 incorporates WCAG 2.0)

Sources:

- [Understanding 2.3.1, WCAG 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/UNDERSTANDING-WCAG20/seizure-does-not-violate.html)
  (no longer maintained);
- [Understanding 2.3.1, WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)
  (updated 17 Sep 2025);
- techniques [G19](https://www.w3.org/TR/2016/NOTE-WCAG20-TECHS-20161007/G19),
  [G176](https://www.w3.org/TR/2016/NOTE-WCAG20-TECHS-20161007/G176) and
  [G15](https://www.w3.org/TR/2016/NOTE-WCAG20-TECHS-20161007/G15). No
  failure techniques are documented.

**The criterion.** "Web pages do not contain anything that flashes more
than three times in any one second period, or the flash is below the
general flash and red flash thresholds."

**Below the threshold** (it passes) if any of these is true:

1. "no more than three general flashes and / or no more than three red
   flashes within any one-second period"; or
2. "the combined area of flashes occurring concurrently occupies no more
   than a total of .006 steradians within any 10 degree visual field on
   the screen (25% of any 10 degree visual field on the screen) at typical
   viewing distance".

**General flash.** "a pair of opposing changes in relative luminance of
10% or more of the maximum relative luminance where the relative luminance
of the darker image is below 0.80". WCAG 2.2 adds "(1.0)" after
"maximum". A pair of opposing changes is "an increase followed by a
decrease, or a decrease followed by an increase".

**Transitions (Note 2).** "A transition is the change in relative
luminance ... between adjacent peaks and valleys in a plot of relative
luminance ... measurement against time. A flash consists of two opposing
transitions." G19 Note 2: seven transitions are 3.5 flashes, which "is
more than the allowed three flashes (six transitions)". G19's test: no
more than three flashes in any 1-second period, and with exactly three,
"the Light/Dark status at the end of the 1-second period is the same as at
the start".

**Red flash.** "any pair of opposing transitions involving a saturated
red". The working definition differs by version:

- **WCAG 2.0, Note 3:** "for either or both states involved in each
  transition, R/(R+ G + B) >= 0.8, and the change in the value of
  (R-G-B)x320 is > 20 (negative values of (R-G-B)x320 are set to zero) for
  both transitions."
- **WCAG 2.2:** "one transition is either to or from a state with a value
  R/(R + G + B) that is greater than or equal to 0.8, and the difference
  between states is more than 0.2 (unitless) in the CIE 1976 UCS
  chromaticity diagram" (ISO 9241-391), with u'v' computed from linear
  RGB.

**Area.**

- WCAG 2.0, Note 1: "a 341 x 256 pixel rectangle anywhere on the displayed
  screen area when the content is viewed at 1024 x 768 pixels will provide
  a good estimate of a 10 degree visual field".
- WCAG 2.2: "the combined area of flashing becomes 341 x 256 CSS pixels,
  or a flashing area of 87,296 CSS pixels". 25% of that is 21,824 CSS px,
  G176's "small safe area" for a single flashing area.
- "The combined area of flashes occurring concurrently and contiguously
  means the total area that is actually flashing at the same time."

**Exception.** "Flashing that is a fine, balanced, pattern such as white
noise or an alternating checkerboard pattern with 'squares' smaller than
0.1 degree ... does not violate the thresholds." One CSS pixel is about
0.0213 degrees, so 0.1 degree is about 4.7 CSS px.

**How to analyze (2.2).**

- "Content should be analyzed at the largest scale at which a user may
  view the content, and at the standard zoom level of the user agent. For
  example, with a video that may play in an area of a web page and also at
  full screen, the video should be analyzed for risks at full screen."
- "For short clips that might be looped (such as GIF animations), the
  content should be analyzed while looping."
- For non-sRGB and HDR video, ITU-R BT.1702 applies: a change of 20 cd/m²
  where the darker image is below 160 cd/m², or Michelson contrast 1/17
  for HDR.

**Relative luminance.** The sRGB formula. Its linearization threshold is
0.03928 in 2.0 and 0.04045 in 2.2, which "has no practical effect".

**Tools (Note 4).** "Tools are available that will carry out analysis
from video screen capture. However, no tool is necessary ... if flashing
is less than or equal to 3 flashes in any one second." G15 names PEAT and
Harding FPA as resources, with "no endorsement implied".

**2.3.2 (AAA)** forbids any flashing over 3 per second, whatever its size
or brightness. It is not part of Section 508.

**Left open by WCAG:**

- sliding versus fixed one-second windows (we would use sliding: the
  stricter reading, and what tools do);
- whether general and red flashes add up ("and / or");
- how to add up separate flashing areas;
- viewing distance, which "the specification cannot account for".

## 3. Other sources

- **ICT Testing Baseline 3.1,
  [9.A-Flashes](https://ictbaseline.access-board.gov/web-baselines/09Flashing/).**
  The steps:
  - set standard zoom;
  - test the larger version (full screen) and the looping version, if
    offered;
  - pass if the flash rate is ≤ 3 Hz, or else if the flashing area fits a
    341 × 256 px rectangle at 1024 × 768, has no general flash, or has no
    saturated-red transition.

  It names no tool and says "There is no reliable, freely or widely
  available solution for determining the resulting flash frequency". 3.A
  Non-Interference ANDs it with the 2.x and keyboard-trap results.
- **No ACT rule exists** for 2.3.1 or 2.3.2 ("No ACT Rules available for
  this criterion yet").
- **DHS** has no ACT verdicts on flashing (act-tt data), and there are no
  Baseline Alignment cases.
- **Tools:**

  | Tool | Status | Input | Notes |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | PEAT (Trace Center, UMD) | Free; a proprietary engine (Cambridge Research Systems); commercial media use prohibited; v1.6 (2017) | Captured or imported video (.AVI) | WCAG 2.0 general and red flash; Windows only; "CAPTURE ... does not always work" |
  | Harding FPA | Commercial | Broadcast video | Ofcom, ITU, NAB-J, ISO |
  | [EA IRIS](https://github.com/electronicarts/IRIS) | Open source, BSD-3-Clause, C++ | Video files | Luminance and red flashes, patterns; transition at ±0.1 luminance, red at ≥ 20; "more than 3 flashes per second in any given second" fails; "not intended to guarantee, certify or otherwise validate" |
  | [Neuroshield](https://github.com/OMARVII/Neuroshield) | MIT, a Chrome extension, one author | Live page | WCAG general and red flash on a 32 × 32 downsample; GIF/WebP and scripted color changes not analyzed |

  IRIS's thresholds match WCAG's, and its license would allow reading it
  as a reference. It is a native C++ build, which would need approval to
  install system-wide. A JavaScript or Python implementation of the
  formulas above is small.
- **Ofcom's** guidance page refused the fetch (403) and is not verified.
  The figures from ITU-R BT.1702 are quoted in Understanding 2.2.

## 4. Experiments (Linux, Playwright 1.63)

Test page `pages/flash/f.html` has five 200 × 200 boxes:

- a CSS `steps()` animation at 5 flashes/s;
- `setInterval` toggling every 80 ms (6.25/s);
- `requestAnimationFrame` toggling every frame (about 30/s);
- a 1 s CSS animation (1/s);
- a GIF alternating every 100 ms (5/s).

Media come from `make_flash_media.py`.

| Method | Frames | CSS 5/s | Interval 6.25/s | Every frame ~30/s | Slow 1/s | GIF 5/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Real-time screenshots (shell or full) | 27–28 fps | 4.8 | 6.2 | **1.2–2.5** | 0.8 | — |
| CDP screencast | 55 fps | 4.8 | 6.2 | 25 | 0.8 | — |
| **Stepped:** Playwright clock installed and paused, `run_for(17 ms)`, Web Animations paused with `currentTime` set per step, one screenshot per step | 120 steps (2 s of page time) in 4.1 s | 4.9 | 6.13 | 27.2–27.5 (29.4 expected at 17 ms steps) | 0.98 | 10 (wrong: a GIF runs in real time) |
| **Decoded GIF** (`ImageDecoder`) | every frame | | | | | 100 ms black, 100 ms white: exactly 5 |
| **Decoded video** (a tool page, played at 0.5× with `requestVideoFrameCallback`) | every frame (61 of 61) | | | | | WebM strobe: 5.0 (5.08–5.17 at 1×, one frame dropped) |

What this shows:

1. **Real-time sampling is unsafe.** Screenshots aliased a 30/s strobe
   down to 1–2.5/s, and a reading under 3/s looks like no flashing: a
   false DOES NOT APPLY. Screencast is better but drops frames. Neither
   can rule out flashing.
2. **Stepping is exact for anything driven by the page's clock**: CSS and
   Web Animations, CSS transitions, timers, `requestAnimationFrame`
   (canvas and WebGL included). It must not run while real-time media
   play: GIFs and videos have to be decoded instead.
3. **Decoding is exact for images and video.** Video at 0.5× costs twice
   its analyzed length, so it needs a cap. Bytes come from the HAR, or the
   2.x media store.
4. **Not yet tested:**
   - SVG SMIL animations (`SVGSVGElement.pauseAnimations` and
     `setCurrentTime` exist);
   - `<marquee>` (not a Web Animation);
   - cross-origin video in an iframe (decoding it in a tool page needs
     its bytes);
   - streaming players (as in 2.A, not performed);
   - the area computation on real layouts;
   - pass T's 0.5 s frames as a trigger. They cannot see fast flashing,
     so the triggers must come from evidence instead (section 5).

## 5. A possible design (for discussion, not a spec)

**Reuse from 2.x:**

- pass T and its browser (full Chromium, autoplay);
- the HAR and the media store;
- `ImageDecoder`;
- the region and frame machinery;
- the scrolled watch;
- full Chromium for iframes (Q11).

**Triggers** (cheap, so a page with nothing fast pays almost nothing):

- an animation or transition with a keyframe step under 334 ms that
  changes color, background, opacity, visibility or filter;
- a timer with a period under 334 ms, or a `requestAnimationFrame` loop,
  that changes styles or a canvas (the 2.x timer hook);
- an animated image with frame delays under 334 ms whose frames differ in
  luminance;
- any playing video;
- a 2 s screencast beside pass T, as a broad net: any pixel alternating
  faster than 3/s.

**Measurement** of the triggered regions:

- stepped capture at 60 frames/s for 3 s of page time (about 6 s wall);
- decoded frames for images and video, video at full-screen scale when
  it can go full screen (2.2), up to a cap.

**Computation**, per 4 × 4 CSS px cell:

- relative luminance and the red test, under both the 2.0 and 2.2
  definitions;
- transitions between peaks and valleys of at least 0.1 with the darker
  state below 0.80;
- flashes in sliding 1 s windows;
- the concurrently flashing area within any 341 × 256 CSS px window,
  against 25% (21,824 CSS px);
- the fine-pattern exception: cells under 0.1 degree, in a balanced
  pattern.

**Output:** the TT outcome (O1), plus the analysis as evidence: where it
flashes, flashes per second, area, red or general, and frames.

## 6. Questions for Abby

- **O1. The outcome when flashing is found.** The TT text names NOT
  TESTED and gives 3.A no PASS or FAIL.
  - (a) **Recommended.** Follow the TT: NOT TESTED, with our 2.3.1
    analysis as a prominent note. When a threshold is exceeded, the note
    says "this flashing exceeds WCAG 2.3.1's general (or red) flash
    threshold; it can cause seizures; treat it as a failure until a
    certified process can test it". When it is below, the note says so.
    SCRT export stays a TT result.
  - (b) Go beyond the TT: PASS or FAIL by the 2.3.1 thresholds, labeled
    "beyond the TT process". It is more useful, but it is an outcome no
    Trusted Tester can give, and it needs a CLAUDE.md change (principles
    2 and 7).
  - (c) As (a), but with a separate "WCAG 2.3.1 (not part of the TT)"
    finding beside 3.A, carrying PASS or FAIL. The TT result stays
    untouched, and the WCAG result is visible.
- **O2. What counts as "flashing content found"** (and so not DOES NOT
  APPLY). The TT says "rapidly alternates between two or more states that
  vary significantly in contrast".
  - (a) **Recommended.** More than 3 general or red flashes in any 1 s
    window, anywhere, any size: G19 and 2.3.2's line, where blinking ends
    and flashing begins (Understanding 2.2.2).
  - (b) Any flash at any rate. Every two-state blink would then be NOT
    TESTED, which is too broad.
- **O3. WCAG version.** Section 508 incorporates WCAG 2.0.
  - (a) **Recommended.** Use 2.0's red definition and 1024 × 768 framing
    for the result, and report 2.2's too when they differ.
  - (b) 2.2 only.
- **O4. Video.** Analyze at most the first N seconds (proposed 10 s,
  costing 20 s of decoding) at full-screen scale; beyond the cap, NEEDS
  REVIEW with the length.
- **O5. Order.** Build 3.A after 2.B (it needs pass T, the media store and
  the timer hooks), or plan it now as part of the batch.
- **O6. IRIS.** Read it (BSD-3) as a cross-check of our counting on the
  same frames, without installing it; or ask to install it as a second
  opinion in tests.
