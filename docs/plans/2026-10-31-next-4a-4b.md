## How to work with Abby (what this round learned)

- **Proportionate.** Build what a careful Trusted Tester does by hand and no
  more. Anything past that is NEEDS REVIEW or a note for a person.
- **Decisions go into the rule they decide,** never into a decision log.
  Delete briefs, approval sheets and plans once their decisions are in specs.
- **Ask decisions as short multiple-choice questions** (AskUserQuestion), each
  with a recommended option and the reason, grounded in the PDF and the W3C
  guidance. Abby prefers the tool to decide clear cases itself (the agent
  answers look-and-decide questions; only "unsure" goes to a person).
- **Cross-check unclear points against W3C:** the Understanding page for the
  criterion, its techniques and failures (e.g. F54, F42 for 2.1.1), and the ACT
  rules plus DHS's published TT results on their test cases. Fetching works
  from the container (`curl` through the proxy).
- **No vanity timing.** Measure only to hold the 120 s budget.
- **Versioning:** a spec is 0.y.z while drafted; it becomes 1.0.0 when Abby
  has approved it, its check is registered and its fixtures pass.
  `tests/test_rules.py` enforces this.
- **Commit and push after each working step;** say what was verified by a test
  and what is assumed.

## Starting thoughts, for the spec drafts (not decisions)

**4.A, all functionality by keyboard.** The PDF finds functionality with the
mouse (Identify Content, p. 19), then operates it by keyboard (HtT 2). A
proportionate mechanical version might be:

- Candidates that a mouse can use but the keyboard may not:
  - elements with click listeners or pointer cursors that are not in the walk's
    stops;
  - title-attribute tooltips (HtT 2.a; ANDI's "title attributes" feature in
    HtT 1.b);
  - controls in revealed content;
  - tabindex=-1 iframes holding focusable content (ACT akn7bn).
- For each candidate, the question is whether there is another keyboard way to
  the same function (HtT 2.b), or whether the information is elsewhere on the
  page (2.c). That is a judgment; look at whether it can be a look-and-decide
  question.
- Check ACT for 2.1.1 rules with DHS results, for example akn7bn (iframe not
  excluded from tab order) and 0ssw9k (scrollable content reachable by
  keyboard). Use their cases before writing custom pages.
- Risks:
  - Executing every control (Enter on each) is expensive and can navigate.
    Proportionate means sampling what a tester would try, within the 120 s
    budget.
  - A false PASS from "we found no mouse-only functions" when detection simply
    missed them. Say what was and was not looked for.

**4.B, no keystroke timing.** "Continue from Test 4.A" (p. 20). Timing-dependent
keystrokes (a password typed at a set speed) are rare and hard to detect
mechanically. Likely shape: DOES NOT APPLY when the page has no user-activated
functionality (from 4.A's evidence); otherwise a narrow look-and-decide
question on whatever 4.A found, or NEEDS REVIEW with a plain sentence. Check
Understanding 2.1.1 for its timing examples before drafting.
